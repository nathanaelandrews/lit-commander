- Tags: core, disease | n_authors: 18
The ability of cells to adapt their behavior in response to genetic or environmental changes is a fundamental biological process that drives cellular diversity in both normal and pathological contexts, including in tumor evolution. Copy number alterations (CNAs), which can include large-scale changes in genomic ploidy, play a key role in creating adaptive advantages in evolving cell populations. However, tracing these populations across experiments and disentangling the role of CNAs from other sources of transcriptional variability remains challenging and expensive. - Tags: core | n_authors: 8
While biomolecular condensates have been implicated in activating transcription, investigating the specific contributions of condensates relative to soluble proteins has been challenging. Here, we leverage the concentration dependence of condensate formation to compare the transcriptional activity of condensed and soluble MYOCD, a cell-type-specific transcriptional coactivator. Using single-cell resolution measurements, we find that cells with MYOCD condensates have more target gene transcripts than cells with soluble MYOCD. While transcription can occur in the absence of condensates, it is less frequent and produces fewer transcripts.
