- Tags: secondary | n_authors: 14
Abstract Ageing is characterised by widespread cellular and molecular dysregulation including broad changes to gene expression. To study the impact of ageing on alternative splicing we profiled whole-blood RNA-seq data from 318 female twins (median age 61) at three time points over 6-8 years. We observe longitudinal changes across multiple classes of alternative splicing. Notably, intron retention, a marker of mis-splicing, shows a striking increase over time ( P = 5.2 × 10 −5 ). Twin modelling demonstrates that intron retention within an individual is heritable, with heritability increasing from 0.44 to 0.55 over time. Together, these findings suggest that mis-splicing is a heritable feature of human ageing which increases in prevalence over time and is associated with age-related disease.
